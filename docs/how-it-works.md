# How it works

## The plan

The planner is plain code: the same data and the same start date always give the same plan, and it needs no AI.

1. It picks two batch afternoons a week from `batch.preferred_days`, skipping afternoons with a calendar event between `calendar.busy_afternoon` times, and never on a day in `batch.avoid_days`, even when every other afternoon is busy.
2. Each batch dish covers up to two lunches. A lunch eaten more than `fridge_days` after cooking comes from the freezer, so only dishes that freeze can go there. The batch sheet says which container goes where and when to move it to the fridge.
3. Dinners are quick or need no cooking. They are scored toward the week's missing legumes and fish, toward light dishes after a GLP-1 injection, and toward guest dishes on Sunday.
4. Breakfasts and snacks rotate through fixed staples. A `treat` snack lands on Saturday. A staple that uses an avoided ingredient is left out of the rotation, and the plan logs a warning naming the staple and the ingredient instead of failing.
5. A side salad is added whenever neither lunch nor dinner is a salad.
6. The validator checks every hard rule. A failing draft is redrawn with the next seed, up to 400 times.

The app keeps a plan ready on its own: once an hour it checks that a plan covers the next seven days and makes the next four weeks when it does not.

One function, `planner.day_menu`, gives a day's whole menu in eating order: breakfast, lunch with its side salad, the afternoon snack, dinner, then the snacks to nibble on. The week page, the top of the day page, the bot's `/today` and `/week`, and the daily menu reminder all read it, so none of them can leave a meal out. A reminder of kind `day` sends that menu at its time every day, with where lunch comes from and a link to the day's page; it stays silent on a paused day. After the week's shopping day it also names what a dish of that day needs that the week's list still asks for, unticked, with the list's link (`pantry.unbought`): the shop came and went without it. Each day also lists up to three other snacks to nibble on. They are the snack recipes that are not a `treat`, use no avoided ingredient and are not that day's own snack, in a fixed rotation by date, so a day always shows the same ones. On a day with a batch session, the day page also carries that session's whole sheet (amounts, containers and the steps), and the steps of the dishes cooked that day.

Every step in the kitchen is a task on one list (`tasks.py`), with the day it is done and a time: cook a batch session, leave a dried pulse to soak the evening before its session, take a frozen food out the evening before it is eaten, move a lunch container from the freezer to the fridge, go shopping, and every reminder set from the chat. The list is worked out from the plan and the database each time, so it cannot go stale, and each task keeps a stable key while its step stays the same. Every surface reads that one list: the chat's batch, defrost and shopping reminders, the Cook page (`/batch`), the day page, the tasks page (`/tasks`) and the app's `/api/v1/tasks`. What the chat says and what the pages show can never disagree. A task's time is the reminder that says it on that day; with no such reminder, 20:00 for an evening step. A session cooked the day its lunch is eaten starts at the household's `batch.start` for that weekday, 10:00 without one.

A task sits on the day the step is done, not the day the food is eaten. The Cook page goes day by day: each day's sessions with their sheets, then that day's other tasks, the reminders set from the chat included. A session can come before its own plan starts, for example a Saturday that cooks a lunch of the next plan's Tuesday. Every reader of sessions (the Cook page, the batch reminder, the soaking, `/batch` in the chat, the app's `/cook`, the consistency check and the verifier) reads every plan for that day (`Plans.sessions_on`), so that Saturday shows on Saturday. A session's dish only counts for days its own plan still covers.

A reminder set from the chat about a dish can say which step it is, as a last word of its dish field: `dish <recipe id> <date eaten> soak|soak-fridge|cook|thaw|buy|move`. The task carries that kind, so the checks can tell a soak from a cook. A reminder without one is a plain note.

A task is done only when someone says so. Every message that reminds of a task (the batch, soaking, thawing, defrost and shopping reminders, and each reminder set from the chat) carries two buttons per task, Done and Not done; with several tasks in one message the buttons are numbered and the message lists which number is which. The answer is stored with the task's key (`task_state` in the database); only the household can answer. A reminder goes out before the step, so a Not done often means "not yet": a later tap changes the answer, and both buttons stay with the chosen one ticked. An answer given in words ("yes, Saturday's session was done") is stored by the AI with a `tasks` change block, which can also take an answer back. The next morning's day message asks about every task of the last 7 days nobody answered, once each. A Not done, or no answer by that morning for a step a dish still to come counts on, starts a turn the bot asks itself to reorganise the rest of the week, like the weekly review; a Done after a Not done starts one that puts back what was moved for it. A session, a defrost or a container whose dishes were all eaten gets only its buttons. Until someone marks a task done, nothing counts it as done: the consistency check treats it as not made, the defrost reminder, the lunch reminder and the day message say "only if the session was done" of a container from that session and carry that session's buttons, and the AI's facts list every task from 7 days back to the end of next week with its state, done, not done or unknown. Tasks from before the bot first ran with the buttons never had them, so they count as they did then. The tasks counted so are the planner's own and the reminders set in the household group; a private chat's reminders get the buttons but are never asked about.

Each lunch and dinner has 👍 and 👎, one vote per person (the person the page was opened as). Tapping the same one again takes the vote back. The next plans use the household's summed votes: a liked dish scores higher in each draw (two votes at most count), and a disliked one is left out unless nothing else fits that slot. A plan already made does not change.

## Changing the menu

The menu can be changed from the chat (see the bot below). Plans are generated files under `state/plans/`, outside the data repo's git, so a menu change needs no token and is written there directly; keep `state/` on storage that survives a restart, as the database already needs.

- A dish can be put in any meal of any day, and two days can swap one meal or all of them. A lunch that changes leaves the container it shared and is cooked in the same batch session when that is still to come and the dish keeps until its day; otherwise it is cooked that morning. When neither main dish of a changed day is a salad, a side salad is added.
- A pause covers a range of dates in which the household eats off the plan. The paused days are kept in `state/paused.yaml` and the plan on disk is not touched: those days show no dishes on any page or in the bot, the shopping list and the batch sheet leave them out, and the meal, batch, defrost and shopping reminders stay silent (the shopping reminder only when the rest of its week is paused; the soaking on the eve of the first session back still goes). A lunch for a later day that a paused day would have cooked is cooked on the first day back. The pause ends by itself after its last day, and it can be ended early.
- A shift moves the menu from a day on 1 to 4 whole weeks later, so each dish keeps its weekday: the days, the batch sessions and the paused days after that day move with it, and the plan grows by those weeks. The days it leaves are paused, so nothing new is drawn for them. A lunch cooked before that day for a day that moves is cooked again on the first day back. Calendar marks stay with their dates.
- A new draw starts on a Monday. The days before it stay as they are, the earlier plan stops cooking for the days it lost, and asking again gives another draw.
- Every change runs through the validator first. One that would newly break a hard rule (an avoided ingredient, a dinner rule, a weekly minimum, a lunch that would not keep) is refused with the reason and nothing is written. The limit on how often a dish repeats only shapes a draw: a dish someone asked for may go over it.
- The plans and paused days as they were before the last change are kept in `state/menu-undo/`, so the last menu change can be undone once.

## Portions

A recipe is written for one reference serving, what a person eating 2,000 kcal a day eats. A person's factor for a day is their current calorie target divided by 2,000, times the GLP-1 factor for that day of the week if they have one. On a day in the person's `training_days` their calorie target first grows by `training_extra_kcal`. A person's `swaps` put another ingredient on their own plate, in the same quantity; everyone else's plate stays as the recipe says. A batch recipe is one pot cooked for everyone, so swaps never apply to it. Plates show grams and kitchen units ("2 thighs", "½ onion"). When a day falls short of a person's protein target, the app suggests quick extras from `settings.toppers`.

## Targets

Weigh-ins are smoothed with a 10% daily moving average. Calories change by one step at most every `kcal.every_weeks` (four by default), only once at least three readings cover most of that window, and never below the person's floor. Losing faster than `goal.max_loss_kg_week` (0.5 by default) adds food; faster than 1.4 kg a week shows a doctor flag. At or below the goal band the person switches to maintenance, so nobody keeps losing past their goal. With the `binge-eating-safe` pack, a window with any binge never cuts food, and four binges in four weeks show a note to seek help. Editing `kcal.start` resets the target to the new value.

A plan is never redone when the data changes, because lunches may already be cooked. A slot whose recipe was deleted or renamed, or which now breaks an avoided ingredient or a dinner rule, gets a substitute of the same kind (same protein first, then the least used). A lunch already cooked stays as it is, and the log says so. Weekly minimums and repeat limits apply from the next plan.

## Shopping

The list adds up every plate of the week, each with its eater's swaps, so a swapped ingredient is bought only for the person who eats it. A batch dish takes no swaps: its sheet, like its plates, lists one ingredient for the whole pot. Batch dishes are bought in the week they are cooked. With store catalogues, each line shows the first clean product, how many packs, and the price, plus the same at an alternative store. `/list?store=<id>` writes the whole list for one store, which is handy for an online order.

Ticks on the list are saved by the app, so every phone sees the same ones. «I have it» takes an ingredient off every week's list and puts it in a short "at home" part at the bottom, until someone puts it back. The list also leaves off what the pantry already holds enough of (below).

## The pantry

The pantry is what is at home, and it is worked out by the app, so nobody keeps it up to date. Only what comes in is stored:

- A tick on a week's list brings in what that line buys: the whole packs the store's catalogue sells (a 1 l bottle of oil, 0.4 kg of loose apples), or the line's amount when there is no catalogue. Unticking takes it out again. A tick on the phone does the same as one on the page.
- The chat says how much there is ("the oil ran out", "there is a kilo of rice"), and the AI sets it with a change (`pantry` in the [data format](data-format.md#changes-from-the-chat)). That amount replaces what the pantry had. Said before the household's `lunch_time` it is what that day's cooking starts from; from lunch time on, it is what that cooking left.

Everything else follows from the plan and the data, the same way every time:

- Each day the plan cooks takes its ingredients out at the end of that day: the lunches cooked in that day's sessions and the other meals eaten that day, at each person's servings and swaps, the same amounts the shopping list adds up. It takes first what goes off first, and frozen food last. A paused day cooks nothing.
- An ingredient with `keeps_days` goes off that many days after it came in. One without keeps until it is used up. What the chat set with a `use_by` (the date on its label) goes off the day after it instead, and what it set as `frozen` keeps until its `use_by`, or 180 days without one. Taking food out of the freezer is told as a new amount without `frozen`, so its `keeps_days` run from the thaw. The pantry forgets an entry after 120 days, or once a later amount for its ingredient replaces it, but keeps every entry of an ingredient with a frozen entry, or one whose `use_by` has not passed.
- «I have it» stays as it was: at home in an amount nobody counted, never used up.

The list for a week leaves off an ingredient when the pantry, without what that week's own ticks brought in, holds enough of it for that week's cooking still to come, in food still good on the last day it is used. A next week's list counts what this week cooks first. A ticked line never leaves its own list. The list page shows the pantry at the bottom, with what each line of this week no longer asks for, and `GET /api/v1/pantry` gives the same to the phone.

The AI sees the pantry in its facts, across weeks, and is told that nothing else is at home as far as the app knows. The consistency check names food that goes off before any planned dish uses it up.

## The bot

The bot answers private chats with the people in `bot.people`, and the configured group; in the group only those people unless `bot.group_open` is set.

When the bot starts on a version it has not announced yet, it says so once ("Now running version X (was Y)") privately to each person in `bot.admins`, or in the group when none of them can be written to. A restart on the same version says nothing, and a version nobody was told about is announced at the next start.

- `/week` lists breakfast, lunch, snack and dinner for each day on one line.
- Commands: `/today`, `/tomorrow`, `/week`, `/list`, `/weight 82.4`, `/waist 95`, `/craving 7`, `/water`, `/pills`, `/about`, `/new`, `/chatid` (Spanish aliases: `/hoy`, `/manana`, `/semana`, `/lista`, `/peso`, `/cintura`, `/antojo`, `/agua`, `/pastillas`, `/presentacion`, `/nuevo`).
- `/about` presents everything the bot does for this household, built from the settings: the reminders with their real times, whether private chats are on.
- It works in a group, in private chats, or both: set `bot.chat_id` for the group and `bot.private_chats` for private chats. At every start it sets its own command menu and descriptions in Telegram, in English and in the household's language (`bot.sync_profile`).
- In a private chat the AI always answers. In the group it answers only a message that mentions the bot's @handle or replies to it. Telegram shows a bot with privacy mode on (the default) only commands and replies to it, so for mentions make the bot a group admin or turn privacy off in BotFather (`/setprivacy`). The bot logs a warning at start when it cannot see them.
- Voice notes are transcribed with the configured `transcription` provider and always reach the AI, in the group too, because a voice note cannot mention the bot or reply by itself. The bot first writes down what it heard, then answers.
- Photos, videos, GIFs, round video notes, stickers and files of any kind always reach the AI too when the provider is a plugin with `converse()`: it gets the files themselves, with their caption as the message. With any other provider the bot answers that it cannot look at them, and neither the file nor its caption goes to the AI.
- The AI writes Markdown; the bot sends it as Telegram formatting (bold, italic, links, lists) and falls back to plain text when Telegram refuses the markup.
- Each chat is one conversation. A provider that keeps it on its side gets only the new messages, with the household's data fresh every time; the others get the recent history, as before. `/new` starts the chat over.
- Messages sent together get one answer: the bot waits 2.5 seconds after the newest one, and for a voice note still being transcribed it waits until the text is there (30 seconds plus 3 per second of audio at most, the time a slow transcription server takes; a note that fails lets the answer go at once). A plugin provider takes what arrives while it writes into the same answer. When the answer comes back without one of those messages, it is still sent, and the next turn answers what it missed at once: an answer the chat waits for is worth more than one that covers everything. Only an edit of a message the answer answers, made while it was written, means the answer cannot stand: then it is not sent, and the bot asks again with all of them, once. An answer to one message replies to it; an answer to several is sent plainly.
- A change still waiting for a yes loses its buttons, marked as replaced, when a newer change sets the same thing (the same recipe, setting, meal of a day or pantry item), so nobody taps an outdated one. A recipe change lists the steps it removes and adds, not only how many there are.
- A restart loses no message. A redeploy can replace the container at any moment, so each message is stored in the database before Telegram is told it arrived, and stays there until the answer that took it is done. At start the bot answers what was waiting, being transcribed or being answered when the last process stopped. A message whose answer went out just before the stop can be answered twice; none is dropped.
- Edits count: an edit of a waiting message sends the new text, an edit of a message being answered reaches that answer as a correction, an edit of an answered message gets a new answer that says what changes, and an edit that adds the bot's mention is answered.
- When the AI puts a choice to someone (which dish, which day), it can offer the choices as buttons under its answer; a tap is that person's answer, and they can ignore the buttons and keep talking.
- When the AI tells someone to do something at a time (cook on Sunday afternoon, take the fish out on Saturday night), the bot offers each step as a reminder, with buttons: here, in the calendar or No (only «here» without `bot.reminder_calendar`). Nothing happens without a tap, and the offer expires after 30 minutes. A reminder set here is posted in the chat at its time, naming the person, and survives a restart; one about a dish ends with the link to its recipe for the day it is eaten, as the batch, soaking, defrost and meal reminders do for each of their dishes; `/reminders` lists them and cancels one. A reminder the person asked for in so many words ("remind me at 23:00") is set at once, with no buttons. The AI sees the chat's reminders still to come with their ids, and when a change makes one wrong (the dish changed, the step moved) it cancels it and says so, instead of asking the household to delete it. In the group, messages not addressed to the bot reach the AI as context, marked, so a remark between people is never taken as an order.
- The AI can change the menu (a dish, a swap, a pause, a shift by whole weeks, a new draw, an undo), a recipe that exists (taking it out of breakfast, for example, which also takes it out of the days planned from tomorrow on), its own notes about each person, and a short list of settings (reminders, batch days and days with no batch, lunch time, weekly rules, guests, foods the asker adds to their own avoided list, the asker's own ingredient swaps and training days, the shopping stores, its own instructions, a new recipe). A change the person asked for is made at once, and the bot answers with what changed and how to undo it. A change the AI comes up with on its own is shown first, with Yes and No buttons, and made only after that person taps or writes yes. When one answer covers messages from several people, the yes is for the person whose request the change answers (the AI names them in the change block); when that is unclear, for the cook (`bot.reminders_for`) when they are among those who wrote, else for the first who wrote. A yes never goes to someone who did not write. In an unclear turn, a change only a person may make for themselves (their avoided foods, swaps, training days, their pills reminder) is never made in a guessed name: it waits for the yes of the one who wrote and may make it, or is refused. Only that person can tap it. Settings are saved as one git commit in the data repo. Pills, the pills reminder, calories, protein, goals and taking a food off an avoided list are never on the list ([data format](data-format.md#changes-from-the-chat)).
- A plugin provider can also offer to do something outside the chat, such as a pull request with a change to the app that a person reviews and merges. The bot shows it and carries it out only after a yes from the person who asked, or from one of `bot.admins` when the plugin says an admin must approve ([data format](data-format.md#settingsyaml)).
- When the bot addresses a person (a reminder for them, the lunch-pills check, the AI telling someone what to do), it uses their Telegram @handle so Telegram notifies them. It looks each username up at start; `bot.handles` overrides it, and someone without a username keeps their name.
- Reminders fire once each, inside a 10-minute window after their time: plain messages, today's meal with its recipe link, batch days, shopping day, defrosting, and lunch pills not logged yet.
- Reminders of one chat due within 30 minutes of each other go out as one message, at the earliest of their times: the app's own reminder of that time takes in the ones set from the chat (and comes forward when one of them is due first), and those set from the chat go together. Each step keeps its own numbered line and its own Done and Not done buttons, and each person is named once at the top. Nothing goes out later than its own time; one due later in the window goes out early with the rest. A new reminder set for about the same time as another joins it, and the chat line says so; one that repeats the same words at about the same time is not set again. When a time still holds several of the chat's reminders (different answers wrote them, so they can repeat and contradict each other), or one counts on a soak, thaw or session nobody marked done, or on food that came in during the last week and that the pantry no longer has, one AI pass writes that message again as one consistent message from the state as it is then and from what the chat said since those reminders were set: repeats gone, contradictions settled, an "if" where a step it counts on is not confirmed. It starts up to 30 minutes before the message goes out and may take that time, less a minute (never more than one review, `llm.review_timeout_s`), and the provider is told the same limit, so its job stops when the app stops waiting. It covers the message as it will go out: the app's own reminder of that time and every chat reminder it takes in. Each of its steps has its own Done and Not done buttons, which mark every reminder that step says; a reminder whose step no longer holds is cancelled once the message is out. When that pass fails or runs out of time, or the reminders changed since, they go out as they were set. A single reminder with nothing against it in the state goes out as it was set; one the chat made pointless later (a thaw after "nothing gets frozen") is for the answer to that message to cancel. The answer is not held back for any of this: the reviewer does not send an answer back for setting a reminder next to another.
- After every change the bot makes (a menu change, a settings change, a reminder set) and once every morning, it runs the consistency check (below). A reminder about a dish the menu no longer has is cancelled there and then, with one line in its chat; a reminder whose time comes before the check is checked as it fires. When a change left a problem that was not there before, one short line under the answer names it. The morning run is at `bot.checkup_at` (08:00 by default, `""` turns it off) and posts no list of its own: what is wrong in the plan (what the plan check found, and the problems the consistency check is sure of) reaches `bot.checkup_chat` (the group when unset) once, as the plan check's turn, where the chef proposes the fixes. When no turn can come (no AI provider, or none by 2 hours later: the check could not run, or the checkup chat is a private chat of no one in the household), the problems the consistency check is sure of go there as one list instead, once.

The AI gets the household, today's and tomorrow's plates, the menu of this week and the next with the paused days, the recipe list, this week's shopping list with what is ticked and what is at home, the food bought that no dish uses any more (it is told to put it back in the menu or say how to keep it, never to let it go to waste), and the last week of logs. So after a shop it knows what was not found and can fit the menu to it. In a private chat it sees only the asker's own pills, notes, weigh-ins and logs. In the group it leaves out weigh-ins, binges, cravings, nausea and notes unless `bot.share_logs_in_group` is set. Group messages are kept as context only when the AI is asked (`bot.history_scope: all` keeps them all). It is told never to change a dose and never to suggest eating less after a binge.

Everything the AI sees goes to the provider you configure. With a local model nothing leaves your network. A provider with web search can find a video or a recipe for a dish; it is told to compare it with your recipe and say what differs. It is also told to look up a fact the household's data does not hold (a nutrition guideline, what a food contains), answer with the figure and its source, and only then say what a clinician should confirm, instead of sending the person to ask their doctor.

## The consistency check

The plan, the batch days, the cook dates and the chat's reminders are written at different times, by the planner, the chat and people editing the data, so they can drift apart. `doctor.problems(data, db, today)` reads them all and lists what no longer fits, with what to change. It is plain code with no AI, and it only reads. It looks from today to the end of next week, the menu the AI sees:

- **A session on a day that is not a batch day.** A batch session still to come on a day in `batch.avoid_days`, or on a day that is not in `batch.preferred_days` while the batch day the planner would pick for that half of the week (Monday to Wednesday, Thursday to Sunday) has no session. A one-off session beside the usual one is left alone, and so is one the planner moved because the batch day's afternoon was busy. Sessions cooked the morning a lunch is eaten, and the ones a pause moved to the first day back, are not batch sessions.
- **One dish cooked twice.** One recipe is lunch on two days in a row, cooked on two different days, when one cook still to come keeps for both. The Cook page shows it as two dishes.
- **A lunch that does not keep.** A lunch eaten today or later is cooked longer before its day than the recipe keeps in the fridge, and it does not freeze.
- **A lunch nobody cooked.** A lunch eaten today or later says it was cooked on a day already past, and no session of that day cooked that dish for that day, or someone marked that session not done. The Cook page never showed it on that day, so nobody made it.
- **A dish that counts on a session after it.** A dish whose recipe has `ahead` steps for the cooking session before its day, when no session comes in the 3 days before (a session on that day counts as after the meal), and no reminder has someone do it at home instead.
- **Prep with no reminder.** A dish with an `ahead` step before the meal and no reminder linked to that dish and day, set or already sent in any chat, comes in time. When it is already too late, the fix it gives is to move the dish. Lunches cooked in a batch session are left out: the batch sheet and the batch reminder carry them.
- **A broken chain.** Some steps only work in order, so the check follows them through the task list, each step with its time. A reminder set from the chat counts by the step it names (`soak`, `soak-fridge`, `cook`, `thaw`); one that names no step counts as none, and the problem says to list it again with its step.
  - **A lunch with no session.** A lunch counting on a session before its day, with that day still to come, when no session cooks it for that day.
  - **A dried pulse nobody cooks.** A dish with an ingredient marked `soak` (chickpeas, beans; lentils are not marked) and nothing that cooks it before the meal: a session cooking the dish for that day, a reminder of kind `cook`, or the meal itself when the recipe is cooked rather than assembled and has no `ahead` steps.
  - **A dried pulse nobody soaks.** That cook with no soak in the 24 hours before it: the app's own soak the evening before the session, or a reminder of kind `soak`.
  - **A soak left too long.** A soak before the cook but more than 24 hours before it. A reminder of kind `soak-fridge`, soaking in the fridge, may take 48 hours.
  - **A thaw left too long.** A thaw (the app's own, a container out of the freezer, a reminder of kind `thaw`) whose cooking comes more than 48 hours later: the session cooking the dish, a reminder of kind `cook`, or the meal. Moving a session moves the cook, so a thaw set for the old session shows up here. A move to the fridge or the freezer is not checked.

  Each problem gives the dates, the times and the recipe id, and the reminder field that fixes it.

  A session, soak, thaw or move of the last 7 days counts only once someone marked it done. One marked not done counts as never made, so the chain shows what it leaves missing.
- **A step nobody confirmed.** A step of the last 7 days that nobody marked (a cooking session however long ago, while a lunch it cooked is still to come), when a dish still to come counts on it: until someone says, it counts as not made, and this one problem stands for what it would break. The morning message already asks about it with buttons and starts its own turn, so the plan check leaves it out; the AI and the verifier of each answer see it.
- **A reminder about a dish the menu dropped.** A chat reminder linked to a dish and its day, when that day's menu no longer has the dish. The app cancels these itself.
- **A recipe nobody can have.** A recipe on some meal that uses a food someone avoids. The planner never picks it, so it never reaches the menu and nothing else looks at it again: swap that food or retire the recipe with `meals: []`. The line does not say who avoids the food, and it is left out of the plan check (it stays true until someone edits the recipe); the AI sees it every turn.
- **Food that goes off in the pantry.** Food the pantry holds that goes off, by its `keeps_days` or what the chat said of it (a use-by, frozen), before the plan cooking as planned up to the end of next week uses it up. All of a food that goes off on the same day counts together, and a leftover under half a serving of the recipe that uses the least of that food is not counted: it is what a pack leaves over once the dishes took theirs.
- **Food bought and on no dish**, as the AI already sees it, without the food the line above already names.

The AI gets what the check found in its facts as "Problems the app found: fix them", each with the menu change that fixes it. Food bought and on no dish keeps its own section there, and is never posted in the chat by the check: once it is frozen it can stay true for days. Reminders about a dish the menu dropped stay out of it, because they may belong to another chat, a private one; the app cancels them and says so in their own chat. The AI also gets the limits the app's validation enforces on what a change may set (`sessions_per_week` 1 to 2, a recipe's `fridge_days` 0 to 4, a shift of 1 to 4 weeks, and so on), read from the code that enforces them, so it never proposes a value the app refuses.

## The verifier

Every answer that changes anything is reviewed once it has gone out: any change block (the menu, a recipe, the pantry, a setting, notes, a task marked done or not done), and reminders it offers or cancels. An answer that changes nothing is not reviewed, and neither is the answer of a turn the bot asks itself (a follow-up, the weekly review, the morning turn about the plan check, a step not done): those turns never bring another one. Nobody asked for what such a turn changes, so its change block keeps only the menu, the pantry and what was done of a step; a recipe, a new ingredient, a setting or a note is dropped, and a change it cannot make is told only to the AI. The answer is sent as soon as it is written, and the review runs in the background on what was sent and on the state it left.

The review reads the whole rest of this week and the next as the answer leaves them, not only what it changes: the menu, the cooking sessions, the full text of every recipe on it, every task with what the household said of it (done, not done, unknown), the pantry and what is going off, every problem the consistency check finds (the soak, cook and thaw chains among them) and each person's health record. The problems that were already there before the answer are given as context: the review counts one only when the answer touches it (the same day, dish, session or step) or could fix it in passing, so a standing problem the answer cannot fix does not bring a follow-up to every answer. The turns the bot asks itself about the week count them all. A step nobody marked stays out of an answer's review: the morning's day message asks about it, and its own turn reorganises the week.

1. **Structural, plain code.** A change still waiting for a yes is made on a copy of the data folder, and the consistency check runs on the copy, with the reminders the answer offers counted as set; a change already made is checked on the data itself, against the problems taken just before it was made. What it finds that was not there before is the answer's doing, and so is a change the app would refuse.
2. **Judgment, a review job through the same AI provider** (`complete()`; it can be a session that reads files and the web). Checks answered each on its own: does it match what the household asked for (each person's notes and chat notes, the household's own instructions, the weekly rules); the timeline (what is cooked when, which session prepares what, what is soaked, defrosted or cooked ahead, each with a reminder at the right time); health (the health export's allergies, intolerances, diet rules, medicines' food rules and fasting). A fourth catches plain nonsense, such as a step with equipment the kitchen does not have, an oven step without the air-fryer alternative, or ultra-processed food where real food would do. A fifth reads each recipe's steps as someone cooking it for the first time with nobody to ask, and flags what they could get wrong: an ingredient that goes in only in part with no word on the rest, an amount per serving next to a total, a size or point of doneness nobody can tell. When the answer moves a dish, a session or a step, or a step was not done, the timeline says how to reorganise the rest of the week.

When either finds something that would go wrong in the kitchen or for health (a structural problem, or a timeline, health or nonsense finding; wishes and clarity are polish the AI still sees but that bring no message), the bot takes one turn of its own that fixes it, for the person whose request the answer answered: one short follow-up message with only the corrections, the wrong reminders cancelled and set again with the right words and time, those that counted on something that will not happen cancelled, and a menu change offered with Yes and No for that person's tap. The follow-up is never reviewed, so an answer gets one follow-up at most. A reminder cancelled and listed again at the same time, for the same person and dish, keeps its place with the new words instead of being cancelled and offered again; in a turn the bot asked itself the new words wait for the tap on the offer. The checkups an answer brings count the reminders it offers as set, so a step it offers is never said to be missing. Its reminder offer lists only what it corrects, so the steps of the first offer nobody tapped yet stay there to tap; only a step it lists again gives way. A reminder it cancels together with a menu change goes with that change's yes. Reviews coalesce: an answer sent while a review runs is not reviewed apart; the review that was running is set aside, and one review covers every answer since, on the latest state. A review waits while the chat has a turn queued or being written, and one that ends during such a turn is set aside the same way, so a follow-up never corrects what a newer answer already changed. A menu change that waits for a yes is read as it is when the review runs: still waiting, it is made on a copy (all of them, in order); taken with Yes, it counts as made; refused, replaced or expired, it is left out.

A review may take up to `llm.review_timeout_s` (default `llm.timeout_s`). When it cannot run (the provider fails, gives no verdict or takes too long), nothing says the answer was checked, and the plan check runs again at the next tick instead of waiting its 10 minutes. The verifier logs only how many problems it found, never their text.

The same checks run on the plan as it stands. Every 10 minutes, when no answer is being written, the bot builds the brief of this week and the next: the menu, the cooking sessions (when each one cooks, morning or evening, and for which days), every task of those two weeks with what the household said of it (done, not done, unknown; the reminders set in the household chat are tasks too), what the cooking reminder says by itself each evening (what to take out of the freezer), what the consistency check finds, and the full text of every recipe on it (up to 150,000 characters; any left out are named, so they are never judged by their name). The judgment runs only when that brief or the checks themselves changed since the last run, so a new or regenerated week, a change from the chat or an edit to a recipe gets one call. What it finds is kept with the brief's key and goes into the AI's facts under "Problems the app found" until the plan changes. A private chat sees only the timeline, the plain mistakes and the unclear steps, since the others can name someone else's notes or health. Once a day, after the morning consistency check (or as soon as a check still running then ends, up to 2 hours later), the bot asks itself to fix what was found, like the weekly review, so the chef proposes the changes in the chat. It does so when the check found something or the consistency check is sure of a problem (a session off its days, a soak, cook or thaw nobody will do, food going off), even when the check's own verdict is empty. Only timeline, health and nonsense findings of the check count, the same problems are never raised twice, and a plan raised once is not raised again; with no turn by the end of those 2 hours, the sure problems go as one list. A plan the app just drew is checked before the chat hears of it: its announcement waits for the check (up to 30 minutes, then it goes saying the review could not run), and what the check finds is raised at once, without waiting for the morning. A recipe nobody can have stays out of this check and out of private chats, since it names a food someone avoids.

## Other chat apps

Telegram is the chat app that ships. The commands, reminders and AI answers do not depend on it: a WhatsApp, Signal or Matrix adapter only has to receive messages and voice notes and send text, and it can be added beside `bot.py` without changing the rest.
